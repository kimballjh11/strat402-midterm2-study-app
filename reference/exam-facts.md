# Midterm Exam 2: exam facts

Source: sources/text/S23_00_Session_Overview.txt (Canvas export, Nov 12 2026) and the equation sheet PDF.

## Scope
Sessions 15 to 22 only: production (S15, S16), barriers to entry (S18), game theory (S19, S20), oligopoly (S21, S22). S14 (AI use) and S17 (project day) are not tested.

## Format
- Paper exam, BYU Testing Center. 59 multiple choice questions, 100 points.
- 25 conceptual questions, 1 point each, similar to quiz questions.
- 34 application questions in short cases, 2 to 4 points each, similar to the spreadsheet exercises.
- Allowed: scientific or financial calculator, scratch paper (do not write in the booklet). No graphing calculator, Excel, Solver, phone. Closed book and notes.
- Equation sheet printed as the last page of the booklet. Same sheet as Midterm 1.

## Practice Midterm 2
14 conceptual questions then 6 short cases (38 numbered questions, 62 pts), about two thirds of real length, same topic weights. External tool quiz: Canvas cannot export it. None of its questions appear on the real exam. Not comprehensive.

## What the professor says he will ask
1. Use a production function to calculate average and marginal product and the profit maximizing amount of an input.
2. Find an isocost line and the MRTS, compare marginal product per dollar, and find the cost minimizing input mix.
3. Identify the barriers to entry and the entry strategy in a real company's story.
4. Solve one shot games for dominant strategies and Nash equilibrium, and sequential games by backward induction.
5. Use present values to decide whether a trigger strategy can sustain collusion.
6. Calculate Cournot, collusive, Stackelberg, and Bertrand outcomes from a demand curve and costs, and use reaction functions to find differentiated Bertrand prices.

## Equation sheet (on the sheet)
HHI = sum of s_i^2 (shares in percent); elasticity = (%dQ/%dP) = (dQ/dP)(P/Q); AFC = FC/Q; AVC = VC(Q)/Q; ATC = C(Q)/Q; MC = dC/dQ, cubic C = f + aQ + bQ^2 + cQ^3 gives MC = a + 2bQ + 3cQ^2, power C = aQ^b gives MC = abQ^(b-1); MP_I = dQ/dI, linear Q = aK + bL gives MP_K = a, MP_L = b, Cobb-Douglas Q = K^a L^b gives MP_K = aK^(a-1)L^b, MP_L = bK^aL^(b-1); AP_I = Q/I; MRTS_KL = MP_L/MP_K; isocost slope = -w/r.

## Not on the sheet (know cold)
VMP = P x MP and the hire rule VMP = w; cost minimizing rule MP_L/w = MP_K/r; isocost equation C = wL + rK; Nash and dominance logic; backward induction; present value of a perpetuity and the trigger strategy condition; Cournot, Stackelberg, Bertrand and collusion formulas and reaction functions; differentiated Bertrand reaction functions.
