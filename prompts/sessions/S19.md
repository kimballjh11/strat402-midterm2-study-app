# Brief: S19 Game Theory I (6 objectives, S19-01 to S19-06)

Sources: S19_S19_Slides_Game_Theory_I (48 pages; pages 1 to 7 are announcements and a previous exercise, mark non-testable), S19_Textbook_Study_Notes_Ch_10_Simultaneous_Move (secondary), reading quiz. There is no in-class workbook for S19 in the folder, the exercise is slide based (Dillard's vs. Nordstrom) and S20_Game_Theory_Exercise Part 1 (Facebook versus Twitter, one shot simultaneous game, ad revenue from 10-K data) belongs to S19 material too: use it here.

Must teach:
- Game theory vocabulary: players, strategies, payoffs, interdependence, simultaneous versus sequential, one shot versus repeated. Why game theory fits oligopoly (reading quiz Q1).
- Normal form: payoff matrix, row player first number, column player second.
- Dominant strategy, iterated elimination of dominated strategies, and the trap: a dominant strategy beats every opposing choice, not just the opponent's current one.
- Nash equilibrium as mutual best responses, the underline method to find it in any matrix, multiple equilibria (coordination games), no pure equilibrium (matching pennies style), prisoner's dilemma and why the equilibrium is worse for both than cooperation. Slides: Visa vs Mastercard (payoffs, what should Visa do, is there a Nash equilibrium, can either improve, could they collude), Dillard's vs. Nordstrom.
- Mixed (randomized) strategy: when there is no pure equilibrium, randomize so the opponent is indifferent. Slides: Princess Bride, employee monitoring. Teach the indifference method for 2 by 2 with a full numeric example. The mixed strategy formulas are not on the sheet.
- Applications to pricing, coordination and advertising (Golden Balls slide is a split or steal game).
Math refreshers: expected value, solving a linear equation in p, reading a table.
Calculation objectives: S19-04 (Nash), S19-05 (mixed), S19-06 (apply). 
Graphs: payoff component (2 by 2 and 3 by 3 editor with best response highlights, Nash finder, mixed strategy calculator).
Generators: random 2 by 2 and 3 by 3 payoff matrices with unique pure Nash (guarantee in generation), dominant strategy identification, iterated elimination, mixed strategy probability in a 2 by 2 with no pure Nash, expected payoff at a given mix.
Practice exam relevance: Q6 to Q8, Q10, Q24.
