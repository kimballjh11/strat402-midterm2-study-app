# Brief: S18 Barriers to Entry (10 objectives, S18-01 to S18-10)

Sources: S18_S18_Slides_Barriers_to_Entry (51 pages; pages 1 to 21 are failure story, S14 AI results and course organization, mark non-testable; testable content starts at "Theory in Practice", the razor industry case, then Learning Objectives), S18_Strategies_to_Crack_Well_Guarded_Markets (9 pages, professor assigned reading), S18_Textbook_Study_Notes_Module_Group_A (Baye and Prince Module Group A pp. 412 to 438: Module 1 Entry Prevention, Module 2 Lessening Competition, Module 3 Restructuring Game Timing, Module 4 Overcoming Network Effects; secondary), S18_Barriers_to_Entry_Exercise_Canvas_latest (Meta, Twitter, Snap data), reading quiz.

Many objectives are conceptual. Expect exam questions of the form "which barrier or strategy does this scenario describe". Use the professor's taxonomy and examples exactly, and teach the distinctions that are easy to mix up.

Must teach:
- What a barrier to entry is and why profits attract entrants. Structural barriers on the slides: economies of scale (with minimum efficient scale), learning curves, brand and reputation, patents and legal barriers, network effects, economies of scope (study guide lists it, check the slides and reading for the professor's wording and examples). 
- Pricing strategies to limit entry or competition: limit pricing, predatory pricing, how they differ (slide "Limit vs. Predatory Pricing"), penetration pricing and where it fits (overcoming network effects).
- First mover advantage (restructuring game timing, commitment) as covered by the slides, reading and Module 3.
- Circumventing barriers: the reading's strategies to crack well guarded markets (reconfigure the value chain, create a niche, leverage existing assets, and the combination point from the reading quiz). Use the reading's own list and examples.
- Exercise: network effects with Meta, Twitter, Snap advertising revenue per user, daily active users, operating expenses as percent of revenue. Rebuild what the exercise concludes in words and by simple calculations.
Calculation objectives: only light ones, learning curve cost reduction per doubling, average cost falling with output (economies of scale), simple limit pricing comparison. Set calc true only where a real calculation is taught.
Graphs: scale component (average cost with MES, learning curve with learning rate slider, network adoption with critical mass).
Generators: learning curve unit cost after doublings; average cost at two output levels showing scale economies; revenue per user times users; limit price versus entrant cost comparison.
Practice exam relevance: Q5, Q13, Q21 to Q23, Q29.
