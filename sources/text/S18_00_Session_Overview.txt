 Strategies to Crack Well-Guarded Markets 

 Read or skim Strategies to Crack Well-Guarded Markets . The most profitable industries are protected by barriers to entry, yet some newcomers still break in and do very well. Two BYU Marriott School strategy professors studied how they do it. 

 Format 
 PDF, 9 pages — opens in your browser 

 Authors 
 David J. Bryce and Jeffrey H. Dyer 

 Source 
 Harvard Business Review , May 2007 

 Sections 
 The Importance of Indirect Assault; The Power of Combination; Picking the Right Combination 

 The big idea: Profitable industries attract about five times as many entrants as the average industry, but those entrants earn lower returns than newcomers elsewhere. The few that succeed avoid a frontal attack. They come in indirectly , where incumbents either can't respond or don't bother to. 

 Head-On vs. Indirect Entry 

 The article opens with two matched pairs of entrants into the same industry. 

 Industry 
 Head-on entrant 
 Indirect entrant 

 Soft drinks 
 Virgin Cola fought Coke and Pepsi for the same shelves and never topped 1% of the U.S. cola market. 
 Red Bull sold a premium energy drink in bars and nightclubs first, then moved into stores once demand pulled it in. 

 Video games 
 Microsoft's Xbox took on Sony and Nintendo directly and lost about $4.5 billion in five years. 
 Jakks Pacific built cheap plug-and-play games for kids and casual players and earned roughly 15% operating margins. 

 The Three Building Blocks 

 Approach 
 What it means 
 Examples 

 Leverage existing assets 
 Use resources you already have, such as shelf space, locations, a brand, or idle capacity, so entry costs less. 
 McDonald's (Redbox kiosks), Toys "R" Us (Babies "R" Us) 

 Reconfigure the value chain 
 Deliver the product through different activities than incumbents use, often borrowing a model from another industry. 
 Skype, Usana, Netflix 

 Establish a niche 
 Serve a fringe segment that mainstream customers, and therefore incumbents, don't care about. 
 Red Bull, Skechers 

 Each approach is ordinary on its own. The authors' point is that successful entrants combine at least two : 

 Reconfigure + niche is the most powerful pair. A low-cost model aimed at customers incumbents ignore keeps the entrant off their radar (Skype, Usana). 

 Leverage + reconfigure uses what you own in a new way (Walmart and Cott's Sam's Choice soda, Costco Home). 

 Leverage + niche points existing strengths at an underserved segment (Babies "R" Us, Skechers). 

 All three at once is rare (Jakks Pacific). 

 Closing the Door Behind You 

 Once inside, an entrant faces its own imitators; more than 150 me-too brands followed Red Bull. The article ends with ways to build new barriers: move into the mainstream quickly, lock up scarce inputs (JetBlue bought the maker of its in-flight TV system), invest in capacity ahead of demand, build network effects, and ride the experience curve. 

 Questions to Keep in Mind 

 If an industry is profitable enough to draw many entrants, why do most of them earn less than they would elsewhere? 

 What barriers protected Coke and Pepsi, and how did Red Bull get around each one? 

 Think of a company that recently broke into a profitable market. Which combination did it use, and why didn't the incumbents stop it?

 Background 

 For most of the last two decades, Facebook has been the most profitable company in social media. Twitter and Snapchat attracted hundreds of millions of daily users and plenty of attention from advertisers, but neither came close to Facebook's profits, and Twitter struggled to earn a profit at all. When a company earns returns that high for that long, economists start looking for whatever is keeping competitors out. 

 In this assignment you will use quarterly results for Meta (formerly Facebook), Twitter, and Snapchat from 2012 to 2022 to find where Meta's advantage comes from. You will compare user growth, calculate advertising revenue, cost, and profit per user, and break each company's costs down as a share of revenue. Separating the revenue side of the advantage from the cost side lets you ask which barriers to entry explain each one. 

 Then you will look forward. TikTok grew into one of Meta's biggest competitors after 2020, and in 2023 Meta launched Threads to take on Twitter directly. Using Strategies to Crack Well-Guarded Markets , you will consider how a newcomer can break into a market that is guarded this well, and whether Meta's lead is likely to last. 

 The data end in the second quarter of 2022, the last quarter before Twitter was taken private and stopped filing reports with the SEC.
