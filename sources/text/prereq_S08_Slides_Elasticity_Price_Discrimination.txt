Elasticity
Conceptually, what is elasticity?
▪A measure of responsiveness one variable to changes in another variable
▪The percentage change in one variable to changes in another variable
▪A measure of sensitivity (e.g. price sensitive customers)
Types of elasticity
▪Own-price elasticity (OPE)
▪Cross price elasticity (CPE)
▪Income elasticity
▪Advertising elasticity

===== PAGE 17 =====
Own-price Elasticity
When is demand elastic or inelastic?
▪Demand is elastic when OPE < -1
▪Demand is inelastic when -1< OPE < 0
▪Demand is unitary elastic when OPE = -1
What does it mean when we say that demand is elastic/inelastic?
▪When demand is for a good is elastic, consumers are relatively price sensitive
▪When demand is for a good is inelastic, consumers are relatively price 
insensitive

===== PAGE 18 =====
Total Revenue Test
How does the OPE relate to revenue maximization?
1. If demand is elastic (OPE < -1, an increase (decrease) in price will lead to a 
decrease (increase) in total revenue.
2. If demand is inelastic (-1 < OPE < 0), an increase (decrease) in price will lead 
to a increase (decrease) in total revenue.
3. Total revenue is maximized when demand is unitary elastic

OPE for Redbox
Was demand elastic or inelastic at a price of $1.00?
Demand was inelastic at a price of $1.00. Remember that Redbox 
maximized revenue at $1.20. Thus according to the total revenue test, 
increases in price should lead to increases in total revenue, when 
demand is inelastic (-1 < OPE < 0).

Unlike OPE, CPE can be negative or positive. Why?
▪If CPE is positive, then goods x and y are substitutes (Dr Pepper vs. Mr. Pibb)
▪If CPE is negative, then goods x and y are complements (e.g. milk and cereal)
