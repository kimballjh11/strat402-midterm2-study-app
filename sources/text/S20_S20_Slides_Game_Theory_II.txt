=== PAGE 26 ===
Simultaneous Game
Is there Nash equilibrium in this game?
Yes, both companies would lower their fees and neither would an incentive to
deviate from this equilibrium.


If they could both agree to maintain high fees, they could each earn a
payoff of 3. Why wouldn’t this strategy work?

Collusion wouldn’t work in this case because both companies would have an
incentive to cheat on the agreement.

=== PAGE 28 ===
Finite Repeated Game
Suppose, for example, that we played these companies played the game over two
periods. What would happen in the second period?

Because the second period is the last period, both players would treat the last period as one-shot
game as they would not be punished for defecting from an agreement to collude.


What would happen in the first period?
▪ A player has an incentive to break a promise in the second to last period (first period), since there
  is no effective punishment in the last period (second period).
▪ Finite repeated games will always collapse back to a one-shot simultaneous game.

=== PAGE 31 ===
Infinitely Repeated Game
What is an infinitely repeated game?
A game that is played over and over again forever and in which players
receive payoffs during each play of the game.

Is collusion sustainable in an infinitely repeated game? If so,
why?
▪Yes. Collusion is sustainable because players that defect can punish players
 in subsequent periods.
▪This strategy is known as a trigger strategy, which is a strategy that is
 contingent on the last play of the game and in which some past particular
 action “triggers” a different action by a player.

=== PAGE 33 ===
Infinitely Repeated Game
Would it ever make sense to defect from collusion?
▪The decision to defect from collusion would depend on how much the
 firm values money now versus money in the future.
▪We evaluate the value of money in the future using a discount rate.
▪The higher the discount rate, the more each future payoff is
 discounted.
▪A player should defect if the present value of cheating is greater than
 the present value of collusion.

=== PAGE 34 ===
Infinitely Repeated Game
Suppose Kellogg’s discount rate is 10%. Should they collude
or defect?
▪PV cheating = Payoff now + Payoffs in the future
  o15 + (2/i)
  o15 + (2/0.10) = 35
▪PV collusion = Payoff now + Payoffs in the future
  o12 + (12/i)
  o12 + (12/0.10) = 132
▪Since the PV collusion is greater than the PV of cheating, Kellogg’s
 should continue to collude.
