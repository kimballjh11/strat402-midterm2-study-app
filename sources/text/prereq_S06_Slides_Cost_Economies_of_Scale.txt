Competing on Cost
▪Kroger competes on cost. After paying for the groceries, it keeps 21¢ of each sales dollar. 
Whole Foods keeps 36¢.
▪Whole Foods competes on differentiation. Its customers pay more, and that premium 
covers overhead of 29% of sales, against Kroger’s 18%.
▪Kroger wins on volume. It generates $3.50 in sales for every dollar of assets. Whole 
Foods generates $2.50.
▪Safeway is stuck in the middle. Its prices are in the middle, its overhead is close to Whole 
Foods’, and it earns a 1.5% operating margin. Albertsons bought it a year later.
▪Most successful in 2014: Whole Foods. It has the best margins and ROA. Kroger’s higher 
ROE comes mostly from debt.
