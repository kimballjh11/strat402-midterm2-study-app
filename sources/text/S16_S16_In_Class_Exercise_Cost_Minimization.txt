In-Class Exercise – Cost Minimization

As you begin reviewing your company’s production information, you learn that labor is paid $12 per hour and the last worker hired produced 84 rollers per hour. Your company also rents roller cutters and crimping machines for $15 per hour, and the marginal product of capital is 120 rollers per hour.

Is the firm using the cost-minimizing combination of capital and labor? How do you know?

No, the firm is not using the cost-minimizing combination of capital and labor because the “bang for the buck” for each input is not equal.

	MPL/w = 84/12=7

	MPK/r = 120/15 = 8

	MPL/w is not equal to MPK/r



Should the firm increase or decrease the amount of capital used in its production process? Why?

The firm should increase its investment in capital because the bang for the buck for capital is greater than it is for labor. 


If the rental rate increases, what will happen to the cost minimizing mix? Demonstrate on a graph showing the old and new cost minimizing mix of capital and labor. Assume that capital is on the y-axis and labor is on the x-axis. Hint: See figure 5-10.

If the rental rate increases, intuitively, you should expect the company to invest less in capital and more in labor. See figure 5-10 to see a graphic depiction of this dynamic.
